Rivosus Ranch Lake Arrowhead · California

Fourteen hectares of pine, granite and running water, held quietly in the San Bernardino Mountains for more than a century.

Walk the ground

The Land

Cedar, pine and the sound of water.

The property lies in the forest that surrounds Lake Arrowhead, nestled between the communities of Blue Jay and Agua Fria. It runs to 14.16 hectares - a little under thirty-five acres - of mature timber, needle floor and seasonal streams that fall away through the trees and are louder in the spring than you expect them to be.

It is unhurried ground. Incense cedar and Jeffrey pine have had the better part of a hundred years to close over what people once built here, and they have largely succeeded. What remains is a clearing, a few good stands of old growth, the water, and the stone.

In the Trees

The forest kept it.

No road runs through the middle of it. The canopy closes overhead, the light comes down in pieces, and the wall sits where it was laid - a little mossed, entirely sound, holding the same line it held when the first photograph was taken of it.

Water is the constant. The name comes from the Latin rivosus - full of streams - and in a wet year you can follow the sound of it from one end of the property to the other.

The Stonework

Laid by hand. Still standing.

The masonry is the oldest thing on the property that people made. River granite, set without mortar showing, in a low wall with squared pillars, a stepped entry and a chimney at the centre. It was already a landmark when the photograph below was taken, and the buildings it once framed are long gone. The stone did not go anywhere.

The Record

A hundred and ten years of company.

1915–1941 Pacific Electric Railway Camp

The Pacific Electric Railway - the Red Cars that stitched together Southern California - kept a mountain camp in these woods. For twenty-six seasons the clearing filled each summer and emptied each autumn. The stone wall, the pillars and the chimney date from this period; the earliest photograph of them was taken in 1917. The camp closed in 1941.

1944–1974 Pine View Lodge

After three quiet years the property opened again, this time as the Pine View Lodge. Thirty years of guests came up the mountain to it, past the same wall, under the same trees - which by then were considerably taller. The lodge closed its doors in 1974.

1974–Today The Forest Returns

For half a century the woods have had the place largely to themselves. Duff has built up over the old drives, seedlings have become timber, and the streams have run without an audience. The century-old stonework still stands - the one clear line left in the clearing, and the reason anyone looking at this land looks twice.

The buildings came and went. The stone stayed put. Rivosus Ranch
