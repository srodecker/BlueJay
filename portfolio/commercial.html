03  ·  Commercial

Revenue against a resort economy.

Lake Arrowhead supports a year-round resident population and a substantial seasonal visitor economy, both of which generate demand the property is positioned to serve. The commercial concepts below range from very low capital intensity to a full hospitality rebuild.

File RR–2026–01/03 Extent 14.16 ha · 35 ac Concepts 3 Status Concept review

Concepts

Three commercial positions, ordered by capital required.

Wedding & Events Venue

Priority

The strongest aesthetic case in the file. A hundred-year-old granite wall with a standing chimney, in a clearing under mature pines, is precisely the setting the mountain wedding market pays for — and it already exists. The build required is support infrastructure, not the attraction itself.

  • The assetThe 1917 stonework, as photographed
  • Build requiredParking, sanitation, power, level ceremony ground
  • SeasonLate spring to early autumn
  • EntitlementUse permit; noise, traffic and parking conditions
  • Rate per eventTo be modelled
  • CapitalLow to moderate — to be modelled

Boat & Seasonal Storage

Priority

Screened seasonal storage for boats, trailers and recreational vehicles serving Lake Arrowhead. Structurally attractive: recurring contracted income, negligible staffing, low build, and demand that is created by the lake itself. Requires hardstanding, screening, drainage and secured access rather than buildings.

  • DemandLake Arrowhead boat and trailer owners
  • Build requiredHardstanding, drainage, screening, gated access
  • RevenueRecurring, contracted, low volatility
  • Operating burdenMinimal staffing
  • EntitlementUse permit; screening and drainage conditions
  • CapacityTo be modelled

Lodge Revival

Concept

Rebuild the hospitality use the site carried from 1944 to 1974 — a small mountain lodge or inn trading explicitly on the property’s own history. The most capital-hungry concept in the file and the one with the longest path to stabilisation, but the only one that restores the site to what it was.

  • PrecedentPine View Lodge, 1944–1974
  • KeysTo be modelled
  • EntitlementCommercial lodging — full permit path
  • CapitalHigh — to be modelled
  • Operating modelOwner-operated or flagged — to be confirmed
  • HorizonLong
Two of these are near-term. Storage and events both trade primarily on land and access rather than on built structure, which puts them within reach of a first revenue season far sooner than anything in the development category. Both still require a use permit, and events carry noise, traffic and parking conditions that should be understood before a venue is designed around them.
